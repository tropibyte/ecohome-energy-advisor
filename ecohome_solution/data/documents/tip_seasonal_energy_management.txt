# Seasonal Energy Management

Energy needs change through the year. A plan for each season keeps bills low and avoids surprises.

## Summer (cooling season)
- Biggest costs: air conditioning during hot afternoons and evenings, pool pumps.
- Set cooling to 78°F when home and higher when away; use ceiling fans to feel 4°F cooler.
- Pre-cool on hot days before the 16:00 peak, preferably with midday solar, then let the house coast through the peak.
- Heat waves often bring grid emergencies and critical-peak prices. Watch for utility alerts and plan pre-cooling, EV charging and laundry around them.
- Close blinds on west-facing windows by early afternoon.
- Solar production is highest in late spring and summer: this is the best season to move flexible loads to midday.
- Run the pool pump in the morning and midday on solar, not into the evening peak.

## Autumn (shoulder season)
- Mild temperatures: open windows at night and close them in the morning to use free "night flushing" cooling.
- Service the heating system before the first cold snap; replace the filter.
- Check weatherstripping and caulking before winter; small leaks add up.
- In coastal climates, late September and October can bring the hottest days of the year; keep summer strategies ready.
- Solar output starts to fall as days shorten; revisit load schedules so midday loads still fit inside the shorter solar window.

## Winter (heating season)
- Set heating to 68°F while awake and 60-65°F while asleep or away.
- Heat pumps: prefer small setbacks so auxiliary resistance heat does not run during recovery.
- Solar production can be 40-60% lower than in summer because of short days, low sun and clouds. Rely more on off-peak overnight hours for EV charging and laundry.
- Let the sun in: open south-facing curtains during the day and close all curtains at night to reduce heat loss.
- Reverse ceiling fans to clockwise at low speed to push warm air down.
- Insulate and seal: winter is when drafts are easiest to find (a lit incense stick near windows and outlets shows air movement).

## Spring (shoulder season)
- Clean or service the air conditioner and outdoor coils before summer.
- Rinse solar panels if pollen or dust has built up after a dry winter.
- Longer days bring back strong midday solar: move EV charging and pool pump runtime back into the 10:00-15:00 window.
- Many utilities have lower demand in spring; it is a good time to review your rate plan and switch if another time-of-use plan fits your habits better.

## Year-round habits
- Review last month's usage by device and compare with the same month last year.
- Keep a seasonal checklist: filter changes, coil cleaning, panel cleaning, water heater flush.
- Re-check the thermostat schedule at every daylight saving time change, since time-of-use windows follow clock time.
